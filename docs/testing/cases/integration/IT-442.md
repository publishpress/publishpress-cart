# Cart checkout gate: purchase limits and stock

## Scenario

The cart checkout must refuse a cart that breaks a purchase limit or needs more
stock than a product has. The gate runs before the claim, so a refused cart
writes nothing and charges nothing.

## Steps

1. Give a product a purchase limit of one and a paid order for one customer.
   Put the product in a cart and claim the checkout with that customer's email.
   Claim the same cart with another email.
2. Check the limit for a customer who has a Buy now order with the product as a
   bump item, then a paid cart order with the product as a main item. Raise the
   limit to two and add one more paid order.
3. Put two plans of a limited product in one cart and claim the checkout. Raise
   the limit to two with one paid order, then to three.
4. Check a limit of two for a customer whose paid cart order has two lines of
   the product.
5. Sign in as a customer whose earlier order has another email. Claim a cart
   with the limited product.
6. Put two plans of one product in a cart with a stock of one and claim the
   checkout. Set the stock to two and claim again.
7. Check the stock rule and the sales window for a stock of one and of zero.

## Expected result

The claim of the first customer is refused with the limit message of the
product. The cart keeps its revision and no claim is written. The other
customer claims the same cart. A paid cart order counts toward the limit through
its main item. The lines of one product in a cart count together with the paid
orders, so a limit of one refuses the claim on the second line and tells the
buyer how many purchases are left. A paid cart order with two lines of the
product counts as two purchases. The claim with a stock of one is refused,
because the two lines need two. With a stock of two the claim succeeds.

## Edge cases

- A bump item of a Buy now order does not count toward the limit.
- A signed-in customer is matched by account when the email differs.
- A customer without an email and without an account cannot be matched.
- The sales window still closes a product whose stock is zero. A stock of one
  keeps it open.
